A screen reader treats an ordinary PDF as a picture of text. It either reads nothing, or reads a budget table sideways — a cell, then a footer, then a headline. The fix is called tagging: marking up what is a heading, what is a table, what order to read it in, and what each image shows.

Almost no public document library has ever been tagged. Fifteen years of council minutes, budgets, agendas, permit applications and public notices, all of them technically unreadable to the people the rule exists to protect. That backlog is the work, and it is the part that takes months rather than weeks.

The rules

Two federal rules, four dates.

  • 24 April 2026 — ADA Title II, larger entities · in force now

    Public entities with a total population of 50,000 or more, other than special district governments. Standard is WCAG 2.1 Level AA, covering web content and electronic documents. This date has passed, so non-conforming content is out of compliance today rather than at some future point.

  • 26 April 2027 — ADA Title II, smaller entities and special districts

    Public entities with a total population under 50,000, and any public entity that is a special district government. Independent school districts are excluded from that category by the rule's own definition, so a district is judged on its population, not on its type.

  • 11 May 2026 — HHS Section 504, 15+ employees · in force now

    Recipients of HHS federal financial assistance. Community health centres, clinics, social service providers, universities.

  • 10 May 2027 — HHS Section 504, smaller recipients

    Recipients with fewer than 15 employees. Same standard, later date.

“Beginning April 24, 2026, a public entity, other than a special district government, with a total population of 50,000 or more shall ensure that the web content and mobile apps that the public entity provides or makes available … comply with Level A and Level AA success criteria and conformance requirements specified in WCAG 2.1…”

“Beginning April 26, 2027, a public entity with a total population of less than 50,000 or any public entity that is a special district government shall ensure that the web content and mobile apps … comply with Level A and Level AA success criteria and conformance requirements specified in WCAG 2.1…”

28 CFR § 35.200(b)(1)–(2). Read from the Code of Federal Regulations on 6 August 2026.

Two of these four dates have already passed. The next, 26 April 2027, is just over 8 months away. Document remediation on a library of 2,000+ files typically runs 4–8 months. Scoping in Q3 2026 leaves margin.

Yes. School districts are public entities under ADA Title II and are covered by the same web and document accessibility rule as cities and counties. District document libraries are typically large, because board packets, policy manuals and enrolment forms accumulate for years.

WCAG 2.1 Level AA adds twelve success criteria to WCAG 2.0 Level AA, most of them addressing mobile devices, low vision and cognitive disability. The additions that matter most in practice are reflow, so content works at 400% zoom without horizontal scrolling, non-text contrast for buttons and form fields, text spacing, orientation, and identifying the purpose of form inputs. The ADA Title II rule names WCAG 2.1 Level AA specifically, so conforming to the older 2.0 standard is not sufficient. Anything built or audited against 2.0 needs re-testing against the additional criteria.

For public entities of 50,000 or more the compliance date was 24 April 2026, so for them this is the present tense rather than a future risk. Missing a date means non-conforming web content and documents are out of compliance with a federal rule from that date onward, which exposes the entity to Department of Justice enforcement and to private complaints. What that means for any specific entity is a question for its attorney, not for a vendor, and any supplier who tells you they can guarantee you will not face a complaint is overselling. What can be documented is the work: an audit against the named standard, a dated remediation plan, and evidence of progress.
