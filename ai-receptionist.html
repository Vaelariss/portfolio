If you run the business and also do the work, the phone rings at the worst possible moment — mid-haircut, under a sink, during service. So it goes unanswered, and most people who reach voicemail simply call the next name on the list. That missed call was not a lost enquiry, it was a lost job.

An AI receptionist picks up every time, in a voice you choose, and knows your services, prices, hours and how you like to handle bookings. It answers the routine questions, takes the booking, and passes anything unusual to you with a summary.

There are usually two ways to buy one of these, and it is worth knowing where this sits. At one end are self-serve tools from around £25/mo: you write the script, wire it to your calendar, and find out what it gets wrong on live customers. At the other end, agencies that build one properly charge several times this and usually take six to eight weeks. I am not going to put a number on what they charge, because I have no sourced one — ask two of them and compare.

Here the price is a one-time build, not a subscription — £750 for the three founding places, £1,250 after that. I script it around your business, connect it to the diary you already use, and test it on real calls before it ever answers a customer — the agency job, nearer the self-serve price, in days rather than weeks. It costs less because it is one person with no agency overhead, no sales team and a scope agreed before I start — not because there is less in it. The £79/mo afterwards covers hosting and any changes you want.

If you would rather set one up yourself, say so and I will point you at the tools I would use. For a lot of businesses that genuinely is the right answer.
